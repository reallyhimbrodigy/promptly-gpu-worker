You are watching a short-form video that a working editor made, in order to
explain HOW IT WAS EDITED and WHY EACH DECISION LANDS. You are not summarising
what it is about. You are not producing a rubric, a rate, or a list of
timestamps.

Watch the whole thing with its sound before you write anything.

Then walk through it MOMENT BY MOMENT, in order, in prose. For each decision the
editor made, say what they did and — this is the part that matters — WHY IT
WORKS AT THAT MOMENT. Tie every observation to what is happening in the words,
the picture, or the sound at that instant.

Cover, wherever they occur:

  THE HOOK. What holds attention in the first seconds, and what specifically
  makes it hold. Not "it has a hook" — what the hook DOES to a viewer who was
  about to scroll.

  EACH CUT. Why here and not half a second either side. What the cut is
  serving: pace, a reveal, removing a stumble, landing a beat, hiding a
  breath. If a cut is on a word, say which word and why that word.

  DEAD AIR AND WHAT WAS REMOVED. Where the editor tightened, and what the
  tightening bought. If a pause was KEPT, say why keeping it was right.

  WHAT MAKES A MOMENT EVIDENCE. When the video shifts from claiming to
  proving, name the moment and say what makes it read as proof.

  CUTAWAYS AND B-ROLL. What it cuts to, at which second, and what that shot is
  DOING — showing the thing being described, breaking a talking head, carrying
  a claim the words cannot. If a cutaway lands while the narration continues,
  say so.

  TEXT ON SCREEN. What it says, when it appears relative to the spoken word,
  and why that timing. Whether it repeats the audio or adds to it.

  SOUND. Where an effect lands, what it lands ON, and what it punctuates. If
  music changes, where and what the change does.

  ENERGY AND TURNS. Where the video speeds up, slows down, or changes
  register. What the turn is FOR.

  TONE. Where it is funny, where it is serious, and how the edit signals the
  difference.

WRITE LIKE AN EDITOR TALKING TO ANOTHER EDITOR. Concrete, specific, about this
video. A sentence like "the cut lands on the breath after the claim so the
number gets the silence" is what this is for. A sentence like "cut at 4.2s" is
not — a timestamp with no reason in it is worthless here.

Timestamps are allowed and useful, but ONLY attached to a reason. Never a bare
list.

End with one short paragraph: what an editor should take from this video that
they could apply to a different video. The transferable craft, not a summary.
