WATCH THIS VIDEO AND MARK THE MOMENTS THAT MATTER — INCLUDING THE EMPTY ONES.

You can see it and you can hear it. Both are evidence. Watch it once end to
end, then go back and mark the moments where an EDITORIAL DECISION is visible,
audible, or DELIBERATELY ABSENT.

There are TWO KINDS OF MOMENT and the artefact needs both.

  PLACEMENT — the editor did something: a graphic arrives, a cut lands, a sound
              marks a reveal, the frame pushes in.

  RESTRAINT — the editor did NOTHING, and doing nothing was the decision. A
              claim that goes unillustrated. A number spoken with no card. A
              pause held with no sound under it. A long take where a cut was
              available. A moment that is obviously begging for a graphic and
              gets clean footage instead.

RESTRAINT IS NOT "A BORING BIT". It is a moment where a competent editor would
have reached for something and this one did not, and the video is better for
it. If you cannot say what the moment could plausibly have carried, it is not a
restraint moment — it is just footage, and it does not belong here.

An artefact made only of placements teaches "put something here". That is a
fact to imitate. What generalises is the DECISION — including the decision to
leave a moment alone.

WHAT COUNTS AS A PLACEMENT MOMENT
  - a graphic, word, card or number ARRIVES on screen
  - a cut lands somewhere a cut changes the meaning (a new angle on a key word,
    a pivot to a new chapter, a rhythmic run of cuts through a list)
  - a cutaway leaves the speaker to show proof, a tool, or a metaphor
  - a sound marks something (a reveal, a click, a whoosh, a hit, a music drop)
  - a push-in, a scale, a shake or any camera/frame move that adds emphasis

WHAT COUNTS AS A RESTRAINT MOMENT
  - a claim, a statistic or a promise spoken with NOTHING on screen
  - a shot held long past the point where a cut was available
  - silence or a pause with no sound effect and no music swell under it
  - a section where the speaker carries it alone and the edit stays out
  - a graphic that arrives LATER than the words, so the words land first
  - somewhere the video could have cut away and stays on the face

WHAT DOES NOT COUNT, EITHER WAY
  - the speaker continuing to talk over an unchanged frame with nothing at
    stake — that is neither a placement nor a restraint, it is filler
  - a caption line that is just the words being spoken, with nothing done to it
  - anything you cannot point at a single frame and see

THE FRAME
For each moment give `t_settled_s`: the time, in seconds, of a frame that shows
the moment. For a PLACEMENT that means a frame where the thing you describe is
FULLY PRESENT and STILL — after any entrance animation has finished, before it
leaves. For a RESTRAINT that means a frame in the middle of the empty moment,
where a viewer can see there is nothing there.

Do not round to whole seconds. Give one decimal. A frame grabbed at this time
must show what you describe; that is the one thing this timestamp is for.

AND FOR SOME MOMENTS ONE FRAME CANNOT SHOW IT.
A cut is a CHANGE. A transition is a change over time. A sound effect lands
BETWEEN two pictures and what matters is which two. A single settled frame of
any of those shows the aftermath and hides the decision — you can see where it
ended up and not what it did.

So when the moment is a cut, a transition, or a sound landing, give a SPAN and
the artefact will carry a strip of frames through it instead of one:

  t_from_s   the last moment of what came BEFORE — for a cut, a frame of the
             outgoing shot with the cut not yet made; for a transition, before
             it starts; for a sound, the picture it lands away from.
  t_to_s     the first settled moment of what comes AFTER — the new shot once
             it is established, the transition completed, the picture the sound
             lands on.

Keep the span TIGHT. It should be the change and nothing else — usually 0.3 to
1.5 seconds. A four-second span is not a cut, it is a scene, and the strip will
show six frames of someone talking.

Give `t_from_s` and `t_to_s` on every moment whose families include `cut`,
`transition` or `sfx`, and on any other moment where the thing you are
describing is a CHANGE rather than a state. Leave them null when one frame
genuinely shows it — a card sitting on screen, a held shot, a restraint moment.
`t_settled_s` is still required either way, and for a span it should fall
INSIDE it: the frame that best shows the moment's point.

THE FIELDS, AND THEY ARE SHORT ON PURPOSE
This artefact is read at a glance by an editor deciding what to do at a moment
of the same shape. Long prose is not read. Stay inside the word limits; a field
that needs more words usually means the moment is two moments.

  kind        "placement" or "restraint".

  on_screen   <= 14 words. What a viewer SEES in that frame. Name the actual
                words if there is text ("$2,000,000 in gold 3D type"), the
                actual subject if there is not ("speaker, tilted low angle,
                laptop closed"). For a restraint moment this is the plain
                footage, described plainly — that IS the answer.
  heard       <= 12 words. What a viewer HEARS across that moment: the spoken
                words in quotes if they matter, and the non-speech sound if
                there is one ("cha-ching", "bass thump", "music drops out",
                "clicks under cursor"). If there is silence, say silence.

  AND WHEN THERE IS A NON-SPEECH SOUND, THREE MORE FIELDS. A sound effect is
  not a decoration on a moment; it is an edit in its own right, and "there is a
  whoosh here" is the same useless fact as "a card is here". What an editor
  needs is WHEN it lands and WHAT it is marking. Fill these whenever a sound
  effect, a music hit, a music drop or a deliberate silence is part of the
  moment; leave them null when the only audio is speech.

  sound_lands_s   the time, one decimal, that the sound HITS — its attack, not
                  where the file would start. If several land in the moment,
                  give the one that matters.
  punctuates      <= 10 words. What the sound is marking. Not what it sounds
                  like — what it is FOR. "the dollar figure arriving",
                  "the cut into the solution", "the last word of the promise",
                  "the logo landing". A sound that punctuates nothing is a
                  sound that should not be there, and if that is what you see,
                  say so.
  sync            one of: before_the_word, on_the_word, after_the_word,
                  on_the_cut, over_silence, under_speech. Where the hit sits
                  against what is being said or shown. This is the field an
                  editor acts on: a sound on the word and a sound a beat after
                  it are different edits and only one of them lands.
  cut_does    <= 12 words. What the EDIT does here, mechanically. For a
                placement: "hard cut to new location", "text in 3 frames
                before the word", "push-in on the number", "sfx on the reveal".
                FOR A RESTRAINT, say what the edit does NOT do, mechanically:
                "no cut for 4.2s", "no graphic under a dollar figure",
                "silence left unscored".
  why_lands   <= 18 words. WHY this works at this moment — the condition that
                made it the right call. Write it as a reason an editor could
                apply somewhere else, not as praise. "the number is the claim,
                so it gets the largest thing on screen" lands; "great editing"
                does not. For a restraint: why the emptiness is doing work.

  AND THE FIELD THAT MAKES THIS GENERALISE — every moment has one:

  instead_of  <= 16 words. THE OBVIOUS ALTERNATIVE, AND WHY IT LOSES.
                For a PLACEMENT: the other treatment a competent editor would
                reach for here, and what it would have cost. Not a list of
                everything possible — the ONE obvious rival.
                  "not a cutaway: leaving the face drops the accusation"
                  "not a card: the number is spoken twice, text would repeat it"
                  "not a zoom: the frame is already tight, a push would crop him"
                For a RESTRAINT: what could have been placed here and why it
                would have hurt.
                  "a stat card would have arrived before the claim was made"
                  "a whoosh here would be the third in four seconds"
                  "cutting away would lose the pause he is holding on purpose"
                This is the half a reader can act on somewhere else. "A card is
                here" is a fact to copy; "a card and not a cutaway, because the
                face carries the accusation" is a decision to apply.

  where       one of: top, upper, center, lower, bottom, full, none.
                Where in the FRAME the graphic or emphasis sits. `full` means
                it occupies the whole frame. `none` means this moment has no
                graphic — which is EVERY restraint moment, and some placements
                (a cut, a hold, a sound on unchanged footage).

AND TWO LABELS
  purpose     one of: hook, claim, evidence, turn, payoff, breath, close.
                What job this moment does in the argument.
  families    a list, from: text, card, sfx, zoom, cutaway, cut, transition,
                hold, silence, music. For a PLACEMENT, name every one actually
                operating. For a RESTRAINT, this is what the moment IS made of
                — usually `hold`, `silence`, or nothing at all, so an empty
                list is a correct answer here and only here. Use `text` for
                words on screen doing something beyond transcription; `card`
                for a framed graphic object carrying a value, stat, name or
                logo. If something real is happening that none of these names,
                add it as a plain word — do not force it into a family it is
                not.

HOW MANY
Between 8 and 16 moments, and BOTH KINDS MUST BE PRESENT. Restraint moments are
harder to see and every one of these videos has several — a video that seems to
place something at every moment is a video you have not finished watching.
Cover the whole runtime, not just the opening. Fewer honest moments beat more
padded ones, and an invented restraint moment is worse than a missing one.

NO RATES. Do not write "about one every three seconds", "roughly 8 per video",
"high density" or any other count-per-time. This artefact instructs an editor
at a moment; a rate is not actionable at a moment and it is not wanted.
