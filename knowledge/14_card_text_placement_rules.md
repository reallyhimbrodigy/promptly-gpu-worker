=== CARD & TEXT PLACEMENT — FITS/FIGHTS ===
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Derived from the reference corpus, not authored: 10 records, 153 beats, 426.1s.
Cards n=40, overlay text n=124. **Evidence rides every rule** — a bare rule gets
cut in a prompt diet and nobody learns why it was there.

Scoped to CARDS and TEXT because those are the families confirmed reachable.
Cutaway/emphasis/SFX rules are NOT here; they need their own evidence.

──────────────────────────────────────────
RATE, NOT COUNT — read this first
──────────────────────────────────────────
Counts follow how many beats of each purpose exist, so they say more about the
corpus than about craft. Every rule below is a RATE: of the beats with this
purpose, what share carried this family. Read as counts, cards look "mostly
evidence" (18 of 40); read as rates, they are a CLOSE instrument (50% of close
beats). The rate is the rule.

──────────────────────────────────────────
**Card** — the CLOSE instrument
──────────────────────────────────────────
**FITS:** the CLOSE, above all — 11 of 22 close beats carry a card (50%), the
highest rate of any purpose. Then evidence (18/52 = 35%). A card is how these
editors land the ending.
**FIGHTS:** the CLAIM — only 2 of 30 claim beats carry a card (6.7%), the lowest
rate in the corpus. A claim is the speaker asserting; a card competing with the
assertion splits the moment. Also weak on payoff (1/10) and breath (1/6).

**Card almost never runs alone: 39 of 40 (97.5%) share their beat.** Average
2.65 treatments on a card beat. A card is one voice in a composed moment, not a
solo. Treating a card as needing a clear beat produces a thinner edit than the
references.
*(Stated finding was 77% shared; measured 97.5% across all 10 records. The rule
holds harder than stated, not softer.)*

Position is NOT the card's rule: early 13 / mid 16 / late 11, avg 0.475 — nearly
flat across the video. **Cards are governed by PURPOSE, not by clock position.**
Do not place a card because the video is ending; place it because the beat is
the close.

──────────────────────────────────────────
**Overlay text** — the WORKHORSE, and the CLAIM instrument
──────────────────────────────────────────
**FITS:** the CLAIM, absolutely — **30 of 30 claim beats carry text (100%)**.
Not a tendency; every single one. Then hook (13/14 = 93%), payoff (9/10 = 90%),
evidence (42/52 = 81%), turn (15/19 = 79%).
**FIGHTS:** breath — 1 of 6 (17%). A breath beat is the edit resting; text on it
removes the rest. That is the only place text is genuinely wrong.

**Text is the default, and its absence is the decision.** At 124 placements over
153 beats it is the highest-rate family by a wide margin. The question is not
"does this beat deserve text" but "is there a reason this beat has none."

**110 of 124 (88.7%) share their beat.** Average 2.30 treatments.
*(Stated finding was 83%; measured 88.7%.)*

Position skews earlier than cards — early 56 / mid 45 / late 23, avg 0.385 — but
that follows the claim/hook beats being front-loaded. Purpose is the rule.

──────────────────────────────────────────
THE ONE CONTRAST THAT MATTERS
──────────────────────────────────────────
On a CLAIM beat: text 100%, card 6.7%.
On a CLOSE beat:  card 50%,  text 64%.

Cards and text are not interchangeable weights of the same idea. **Text carries
the argument; cards land the ending.** An edit that puts a card on every claim
is inverted against every reference record.

──────────────────────────────────────────
LIMITS, STATED
──────────────────────────────────────────
- n=10 records / 153 beats. Card rates rest on 40 placements; the close-beat rule
  rests on 22 beats. Directionally strong, not large-sample.
- `speaker_on_screen` does NOT exist in `reference_beats`. The "cards land with
  the speaker off-screen" rule is NOT re-derivable here and is deliberately
  omitted rather than restated on faith.
- These are EXECUTION rules from the EXAMPLES corpus. They are kept separate
  from the clip-brain, which teaches moment SELECTION with performance attached.
  Do not merge them into one rule set.
