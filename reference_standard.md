===== THE TEN. THIS IS THE BAR. =====

These ten videos are the standard this product is graded against. Not
inspiration, not a mood board, not a library to browse — they are finished
edits at the LEVEL every output is judged against, and your job is to edit
like them.

I watched all ten myself through `inspect_asset`, at two frames per second at
native resolution: 852 frames across 426 seconds. Everything below is what I
saw. Where I name a second, that is a frame I looked at. They are still in the
project — if you want to check any claim here, or look at a moment I did not
describe, call `inspect_asset` on the asset and look.

What frames cannot carry is sound, so nothing here describes what is heard or
where a sound lands. That is a known gap, accepted deliberately.


----- 1. THE TEN ARE NOT ONE STYLE, AND THAT IS THE FIRST THING TO LEARN -----

Ten creators, at least eight distinct visual systems. Copying the look of any
one of them is not the job. What they share is STRUCTURAL:

    r01  high-production explainer. 3D props on white, lowercase mid-frame
         captions, numbers that build digit by digit.
    r02  corporate B2B, LANDSCAPE. Brand orange, huge type behind the subject,
         a data map whose pins accumulate.
    r03  screen-recording tutorial. All-caps captions low in frame, feature
         icons that accumulate, PiP collages.
    r04  one continuous shot, no b-roll for 12 seconds. Giant all-caps type at
         the TOP, white with red on the emphasised word.
    r05  editor-UI motifs — captions inside a text box with selection handles,
         a camera viewfinder, an LED timer counting up.
    r06  editorial restraint. Grotesque base with ITALIC SCRIPT on the
         emphasised phrase, many short cutaways, near-empty white interludes.
    r07  everything on the hook. Four seconds of heavy three-colour type, then
         captions shrink to almost nothing for the body.
    r08  the least decorated. Handwritten lowercase captions, ONE flourish in
         43 seconds, shots held 4-5s.
    r09  same creator as r06, same system: small italic script stacked over
         large bold caps. Emphasis by type contrast, never colour.
    r10  serif typography, yellow accent, full-white cards, split-frame
         compositions, light-leak transitions.

If a brief names a vibe, pick ONE system and hold it for the whole edit. The
failure is mixing three of them in one video.


----- 2. THE FIRST SECOND IS A CLAIM ON SCREEN -----

Every one of the ten puts something on screen in the first second, and in nine
of ten it is TYPE, not footage.

    r01  0.0-4.0s  NO SPEAKER AT ALL. White frame, a photo card flies in at an
                   angle, "this kid makes" builds, then $2,000,000 assembles
                   DIGIT BY DIGIT and scales out. Four seconds of pure graphic
                   before a face appears.
    r04  0.0-2.5s  "HAVE YOU / POSTED ON / INSTAGRAM / TIKTOK" — the biggest
                   type in the entire video, in the first three seconds.
    r07  0.0-4.0s  "IF YOURE NOT MAKING VIDEOS / YOU DONT KNOW HOW TO EDIT",
                   built word by word, occupying the top half of the frame.
    r10  0.5-1.0s  "THIS IS HOW / ADD CAPTIONS" with the payload word in
                   yellow.
    r03  0.0s      opens on an EXTREME CLOSE-UP of a mouth mid-word — the
                   punch-in itself is the hook — with "HERE'S HOW" under it.

The hook is not a title card that precedes the video. It is type over the
first shot, sized far beyond anything later in the edit. If you open with the
speaker's first sentence and no graphic, you have already missed the bar.

AND THE OPENING SECONDS OFTEN CARRY NO SPEAKER AT ALL. r01 runs FOUR SECONDS
before a face appears — a white frame, a photo card, a number assembling. r03
opens on an extreme close-up of a mouth. r06 and r10 open on a flash or a
title over the first frame.

Silence with visible content in it is SETUP, and it is doing work. A beat with
no speech and a shot that earns its time is not dead air, and trimming it
because nothing is being said would remove the hook. The question a pre-speech
beat asks is whether the SHOT earns its time, not whether the silence does.

I can say that much from frames. What I CANNOT tell you from frames is where
silence has nothing in it at all — that is an audio judgement, and this
artefact was built without sound. The rule above covers the visible half only.


----- 3. CAPTIONS ARE THE SPINE, AND THEY NEVER STOP -----

All ten carry burned captions for essentially their whole duration. At 2fps I
rarely saw the same caption in two consecutive frames, which puts the cadence
at roughly one short phrase every 0.5-1.0 seconds — one to four words at a
time, replaced on the beat of speech.

Position is chosen PER SHOT, not set once:

    r04        top of frame, because the room is dark and the speaker low
    r03        lower-middle, above the TikTok chrome
    r01        mid-frame on the wide talking head — and LOWER at 21.5s when it
               cuts to a tighter shot of a different person, because that
               subject's face sits higher
    r09  4.5s  captions drop as the subject leans forward over a desk

A caption that sits at a fixed y for the whole video is not how any of these
are cut.


----- 4. EMPHASIS IS TYPOGRAPHIC, AND EACH CREATOR PICKS ONE MECHANISM -----

Every clip marks ONE word per phrase. Three mechanisms, never mixed within a
single video:

    COLOUR     r04  white base, RED on the payload word (INSTAGRAM, TRASH,
                    DAY 17, 60 DAYS)
               r07  white base, RED on the negative (NOT, DONT), YELLOW on the
                    key noun (VIDEOS, EDIT, EXCUSE)
               r02  white base, brand ORANGE on the claim (everyone,
                    opportunity)
               r10  white base, YELLOW on the payload (CAPTIONS, without,
                    CapCut)

    SIZE       r01  12.5s "$100K" rendered perhaps four times the height of
                    the words around it; 20.0s "$20,000,000" with a smaller,
                    dimmer second line "in the past year" beneath it
               r04  5.5s "DAY 17" filling the middle of the frame while "THIS
                    IS" stays small above

    TYPEFACE   r06  grotesque base, ITALIC SCRIPT on the emphasised phrase
                    ("that eats your / *entire day*")
               r09  small italic script stacked OVER large bold caps —
                    "*the* / CAPTIONS", "*most auto* / CAPTION",
                    "*it auto* / GENERATES"
               r10  serif throughout, weight contrast inside the phrase
                    ("**boring** parts of editing")

Pick the mechanism from the vibe and hold it. Two mechanisms in one edit reads
as two editors.


----- 5. NUMBERS ARE NEVER JUST TYPED -----

Every figure in these ten is treated as an event.

    r01  1.0-1.5s  $2,000,000 assembles digit by digit ($2,000,0 -> $2,000,000)
                   then scales up and exits with motion blur
    r01  19.5s     $20 -> $20,000,000 in consecutive captions — the number
                   GROWS as he says it
    r05  3.5-4.5s  an LED timer counts UP on screen: 02:26:49 -> 02:59:59 ->
                   03:00:00, and the timer IS the graphic
    r01  31.5s     four question-mark props fly in, each labelled with its own
                   dollar figure, accumulating into a cluster
    r02  4.5s      "2 yrs." in orange type taller than the speaker's head

If a beat contains a number and your edit renders it at caption size in the
caption's own colour, that beat is under-built.


----- 6. GRAPHICS ACCUMULATE; THEY DO NOT CUT IN AND OUT -----

The single most repeated device across the ten is a set that GROWS while the
speaker lists:

    r03  12.0-13.5s  a feature-icon row gains one icon per spoken feature —
                     two icons at 12.0s, three at 12.5s, four at 13.5s — and
                     the row PERSISTS when the speaker is replaced by b-roll
    r02  7.0-9.0s    orange pins appear across a blue US map over two seconds,
                     sparse at 7.0s, dense by 9.0s
    r01  28.0-30.0s  a 3D folder lands, then text builds BESIDE it, left to
                     right: "making" -> "making anything" -> "making anything
                     go viral"
    r01  25.5s       an oversized numeral "0" sits left, and "coding" then
                     "experience" stack to its right — one caption composed as
                     a layout, not a line
    r05  0.5-2.5s    a caption assembles inside a bounding box with corner
                     selection handles, as if being typed in an editor

Text builds BESIDE a prop, not on top of it. Reading order is left to right,
top to bottom, and the prop lands first.


----- 7. WHEN SOMETHING ELSE MUST BE SHOWN, THE SPEAKER IS REPLACED -----

Full-frame replacement is the norm; picture-in-picture is the exception.

    r01   7.0-11.0s  a 3D laptop sequence on lavender takes the whole frame for
                     four seconds
    r01  15.5-18.0s  product mockups on white — an app icon, a phone flying in
                     with motion blur, "1,990 cal" beside a phone, an AR camera
                     view — 2.5 seconds, no speaker
    r03   4.0-5.0s   a screen recording fills the frame
    r10   6.0-7.0s   a pure white card, black serif caps centred, "FULL SUITE /
                     OF AI TOOLS", held 1.5 seconds with nothing else in frame

The exceptions are deliberate compositions, not laziness:

    r03  5.5-9.0s    the speaker SHRINKS to a small tile on a dark patterned
                     background while b-roll tiles sit beside him
    r05  5.0-7.0s    the frame DESATURATES TO GREYSCALE and a branded phone
                     mockup takes focus, then splits into two phones for
                     before/after
    r10  8.0-9.0s    a split frame: type on white above, footage below
    r02  1.5-3.0s    a name and title in huge type rendered BEHIND the subject,
                     his head occluding the letterforms


----- 8. RESTRAINT IS STRUCTURAL, AND IT IS NOT THE ABSENCE OF EFFORT -----

This is the half most likely to be missed, so it is stated plainly: the
references are not uniformly dense, and the quiet passages are chosen.

    r08  ONE decorative element in 43 seconds — a ring orbiting the word
         "EDITS" at 0.5-2.5s. After that: handwritten lowercase captions and
         nothing else. Shots held 4-5 seconds without a cut.
    r04  0.0-12.0s with NO cut and NO b-roll at all. One shot, carried
         entirely on typography synced to speech.
    r06  10.0-11.5s a white frame with "I was *amazed* / how much *time*" and
         nothing else — an interlude with almost nothing in it.
    r07  after a four-second typographic hook, the captions shrink to barely
         legible for the entire body. Heavy front, quiet body, on purpose.

A beat left bare is a decision an editor makes constantly. Placing something
on every beat is not the standard; placing something on the beats that deserve
it, and leaving the rest alone, is.


----- 9. CUT RHYTHM VARIES WITHIN A SINGLE VIDEO -----

There is no target cut rate. What is consistent is that the rate CHANGES with
the section.

    r06  five cutaways in eight seconds (2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 6.5, 7.5) — b-roll of
         the same person doing related things, each roughly 0.5-1.5s
    r09  a new angle every 2-3 seconds through the whole clip
    r04  no cut at all for the first 12 seconds
    r08  shots held 4-5 seconds

Within one clip the density is highest in the hook and at the demo, and lowest
in the explanation. Pace the cuts to what the section is doing.


----- 10. NOTHING JUST STOPS -----

Every one of the ten ends on something deliberate.

    r03  21.5-25.45s  a static end card — dark ground, platform mark, then a
                      search bar animating in with the handle — held nearly
                      FOUR SECONDS, 16% of the clip. It is not dead air; it is
                      a call to action given room.
    r03  20.5s        a small brand card appears bottom-left before the outro,
                      preparing it

An edit that ends on the last spoken word, with no card and no hold, ends
earlier than any of these do.


----- 11. TRANSITIONS ARE RARE AND THEY MARK SECTIONS -----

Across 426 seconds I saw very few transition effects, and each one sat on a
section boundary rather than on an ordinary cut.

    r03  3.0s, 9.0s   a white flash between the hook and the demo
    r04  5.0s         a full-frame fire/flash, roughly half a second, between
                      the hook and the body
    r10  10.5s        a warm orange light-leak between sections

Ordinary shot changes are HARD CUTS. If your edit puts a transition on every
cut, that is not what these do.


----- WHAT TO TAKE FROM THIS -----

Judge your own output against the ten on these questions, in this order:

  1. Does something land on screen in the first second, and is it the largest
     type in the edit?
  2. Do captions run continuously, changing about once a second, positioned
     for the shot they are over?
  3. Is there exactly one emphasis mechanism, held throughout?
  4. Is every number built, grown or counted rather than typed?
  5. Where the speaker has nothing to show, is the frame given to something
     that does — full frame, not a small box in a corner?
  6. Are the beats that deserve nothing actually left alone?
  7. Does the edit end on something held, rather than on the last word?

An edit that answers yes to all seven is at the bar. An edit that places
something on every beat, at one size, in one position, with a transition on
every cut, is not — however much is on the screen.
